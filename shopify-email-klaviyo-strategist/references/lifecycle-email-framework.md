# Lifecycle Email Framework

The customer lifecycle, the flows that serve each stage, and sensible timing. Benchmarks are orientation ranges, not guarantees — always weight the store's own data.

## 1. Lifecycle stages

| Stage | Customer state | Primary goal | Key flows |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Acquire | New subscriber, not bought | Capture & consent | Sign-up / list growth |
| Activate | Subscribed, considering | First purchase | Welcome flow |
| Convert | High intent, hesitating | Recover the sale | Browse abandonment, abandoned cart/checkout |
| Retain | Bought once | Second purchase | Post-purchase, replenishment |
| Grow | Repeat buyer | Increase frequency & AOV | VIP, cross-sell, replenishment |
| Win back | Lapsing/lapsed | Reactivate | Winback / sunset |

A healthy program covers every stage. Map current flows to this table; gaps are the opportunity.

## 2. Flow revenue priority (typical)

Most stores see the fastest revenue from, roughly in this order:

1. **Abandoned checkout / cart** — highest intent, near-purchase recovery.
2. **Welcome flow** — captures fresh interest, drives first purchase.
3. **Browse abandonment** — broad intent capture.
4. **Post-purchase** — sets up retention and reviews/UGC.
5. **Winback** — recovers lapsing customers cheaply.
6. **Replenishment / VIP** — depends on product type and repeat behavior.

Use this as a default sequencing bias; adjust to the store's data.

## 3. Core flows: trigger, timing, intent

### Welcome flow
- **Trigger:** new subscriber (sign-up form / popup).
- **Timing:** immediate, then 3-5 messages over ~5-10 days.
- **Intent:** deliver the sign-up promise, tell the brand story, set expectations, drive first purchase. Front-load value; don't lead with a deep discount if you can avoid it.

### Abandoned checkout / cart
- **Trigger:** started checkout (or add-to-cart) without purchase.
- **Timing:** first message ~1-4h, second ~24h, third ~48-72h.
- **Intent:** remove friction (reassurance, shipping/returns), remind of the item, then optional incentive last. Distinguish "started checkout" (higher intent) from "added to cart".

### Browse abandonment
- **Trigger:** viewed product(s), no add-to-cart, is a known/consented contact.
- **Timing:** ~2-12h after browse, 1-2 messages.
- **Intent:** gentle nudge with the viewed product + alternatives/social proof. Lower intent than cart — softer tone, rarely discount.

### Post-purchase
- **Trigger:** order placed / fulfilled.
- **Timing:** order confirmation feel, then onboarding/usage, then review request (~7-21 days depending on product), then cross-sell.
- **Intent:** reduce buyer's remorse, drive product success, collect reviews/UGC, set up the next purchase. Strong retention lever, often underused.

### Winback
- **Trigger:** no purchase in X days (set by purchase cycle, e.g. 60/90/180).
- **Timing:** a short series with escalating incentive.
- **Intent:** reactivate lapsing customers; the last message can carry a stronger offer. Feeds into sunset if no engagement.

### Replenishment
- **Trigger:** time since last purchase of a consumable, based on expected run-out.
- **Timing:** shortly before the product is likely to run out.
- **Intent:** make reordering effortless. Only relevant for consumable/replenishable products.

### VIP
- **Trigger:** crosses a value/frequency threshold (top spenders / repeat buyers).
- **Timing:** ongoing recognition + early access + perks.
- **Intent:** reward and retain the most valuable customers; increase frequency and advocacy.

## 4. Benchmarks (orientation only)

| Metric | Typical orientation |
| --- | --- |
| Email revenue share | 15%-30% of total revenue |
| Flow vs campaign revenue split | Flows often 40-60% of email revenue |
| Welcome / cart flows | Among the highest revenue-per-recipient |
| List growth from sign-up form | Varies; optimize the offer & placement |

Treat these as sanity checks. The store's own trend matters more than any benchmark.

## 5. Newsletter / campaign mix

Balance three campaign types so you're not always selling:

- **Value/content** (education, how-to, story, UGC) — builds engagement & deliverability.
- **Product/merchandising** (new arrivals, best-sellers, collections) — drives discovery.
- **Promotional** (sales, launches, seasonal) — drives spikes; use sparingly to protect margin and fatigue.

A common healthy ratio leans toward value + product, with promotions reserved for genuine moments.
